# ENKIRIDION — The Trial (Story Plan)
*Dual-timeline story bible: the trial of Socrates (399 BC) woven against Mario Valente's seven-year crucible (fictional). Serves as the GBA game's narrative spine and a standalone novella treatment.*

> Supersedes note: the earlier ENKIRIDION GBA technical/feasibility plan is a separate document. This is the **story** layer that sits on top of it. All names, figures, and events below are fictional; the emotional architecture is the point, not any biography.

## Context

ENKIRIDION's story reframes "the trial" away from a single courtroom. It is a seven-year tribunal in which life delivers the verdict in installments — a family death, a family restaurant sinking into debt, a global lockdown, a love lost, a market war the protagonist believes is rigged and refuses to profit from, an illness in the family, a father breaking down under the weight, and a hoped-for reform that never arrives.

The rhyme with Socrates is not decorative — it is structural:
- **Both are convicted of optimism about truth.** Athens: impiety + corrupting the youth. The protagonist's world: "too optimistic, believed the wrong truths."
- **Both refuse Crito.** Socrates refuses the escape to Thessaly; Mario refuses easy money and the fast-talking sales path despite proof he could win that game.
- **Both counter-propose a seat at the table.** Socrates demands free meals in the Prytaneum; the protagonist is a restaurant son — the counter-proposal stages at his father's table.
- **Both swallow a hemlock.** Socrates: the cup. Mario: accepting that the system he trusted never corrected itself — dignity robbed by design.
- **One structural inversion, named as such:** Socrates has no father standing behind him. Mario's father writes the letter — "I love you, I stand by you no matter what" — during the darkest night of setting up a sinking dining room. Timeline A has an **anti-accuser**.

Design boundaries: fictionalize everything, keep emotional truth; the protagonist carries the author's silhouette, not his biography; tone between raw and mythic; ending = **hope earned the hard way**. Trigger-texture to honor: a nameless late-night monster movie whose line — "my war isn't over" — cracks him open; Marcus's "to bear this worthily is good fortune" as the anchor quote; a small toy talisman; "misericordia est virtus fortium"; "agárrate los huevos y acuérdate quién sos."

## Logline & theme

**Logline.** A South Florida restaurant son, seven years into a private war against a rigged market and a sinking family business, is handed an old device through which the philosophers speak — and every night it drops him into Athens, 399 BC, as the juror history never counted at the trial of Socrates. As the two trials rhyme toward their verdicts, he learns that a jury can take everything except the one thing the charge was really about.

**Theme.** *Optimism is the impiety they convict you for.* Athens killed Socrates for believing in the wrong gods and infecting the young with questions; the world convicted the protagonist of believing the wrong truths and refusing to stop hoping. The examined life is not a seminar — it is **guerrilla warfare against your own despair**. What the crowd's verdict can touch: your money, your name, your restaurant, your years. What it cannot touch: whether you bore it worthily. That distinction — the Stoic dichotomy of control — is not the story's lesson bolted on; it is Socrates's actual bequest, the thing that survived the hemlock and became Epictetus and then Marcus and then the letter the protagonist's father wrote him in the dark.

## Frame & presence mechanic

**Enki** frames everything — the still voice at the freshwater deep (the Apsu = the game's hub menu, "the small tablet that carries voices"). He does not explain the device; he lowers the bucket slowly.

Inside Timeline B the protagonist is **Juror #501** — Athenian juries were 500; he is the vote that was never tallied, present at every proceeding, able to deliberate, argue, even cast a stone — *and it changes nothing*. The verdict is fixed by history. **That powerlessness is the mechanic's whole point:** the game lets you vote to acquit Socrates and shows you the 280–221 conviction anyway, so that when you return to Timeline A you understand the only trial whose verdict you actually author is the one you hold over yourself.

## Character sheet

**Protagonist — "Mario Valente"** (echo, not the name; "Valente" = valiant/brave, an immigrant restaurant surname). 25→32 across the crucible. Carries a tiny plastic astronaut talisman (fictionalized from the toy). His five inner selves are playable *masks*:
- **The Builder** — autonomous, wants proof he made something that matters. Validation-hungry. Speaks in "This time next year…"
- **The Waiter / Laborer** — wants the chains off, freedom of choice. At **war with the Builder** (the spine of the internal tribunal).
- **Scary Dude** — night mode; the one who sets up a sinking dining room at 3am and does not flinch. "My soul is not meant for this."
- **The Innocent Child** — oldest hope; wants prosperity for family and world. The **plaintiff** of the internal trial.
- **The Shamed One** — oldest wound; felt small, poor, cowardly, "not enough."

**The accuser-triad (Timeline A ↔ Meletus/Anytus/Lycon):**
- **"Deacon" — the true believer who curdled (↔ Meletus).** A former friend from the crypto/scam world who recruited Mario and took the refusal personally; now prosecutes him as a naïf who "wasted the golden ticket." The zealot.
- **"Mr. Preston" — the respectable one protecting his interests (↔ Anytus).** An industry/creditor figure (a supplier, a landlord, a family-reputation gatekeeper) who represents the sober world Mario's optimism embarrasses. Not cruel — *invested*. The dangerous one.
- **The Pile-On, given a face — "the Ticker" (↔ Lycon).** The faceless market/media mob that convicted him of hope, personified as a scrolling price-and-comment feed that occasionally resolves into a single sneering avatar. The crowd.

**Crito → "Tani."** The friend with the escape: fast-money schemes, the seedy sales-office riches. Loves Mario, wants him to *just take the win* (he'd already proven, twice, that he could). His Laws speech is met with Mario's refusal to cheat the system he's trying to reform.

**Xanthippe → his mother.** "I am ashamed, but I understand." Disdain braided with trust. Her illness makes **time the true adversary** — the one accuser Marcus can't argue away.

**The Father — the anti-accuser (STRUCTURAL INVERSION, named as such).** Socrates faced Athens with no one behind him. Mario has the letter: *"I love you, I stand by you no matter what,"* written the night he broke down over restaurant debt. Every time Timeline B shows Socrates alone before the jury, Timeline A answers with the father who refuses to be a juror. This inversion is the source of the "hope earned the hard way."

**Brandon → the Plato-figure.** A young server assistant at the family restaurant who "sees the lore unfold" — the witness. He records nothing on purpose and everything by accident; the story we're reading/playing is implied to be *his* account. He is how "my war isn't over" outlives the protagonist.

**Philosopher deployment across the five movements** (from `personas.json`):
- **Enki** — frame, all movements; the final bond.
- **M1 Socrates** — the mirror; present in both timelines.
- **M2 Epictetus** — the former slave who chose freedom; speaks to **the Waiter**.
- **M3 Marcus Aurelius** — the anchor ("to bear this worthily is good fortune"); speaks to **the Builder** exhausted by the ledger.
- **M4 Laozi & Zhuangzi** — the lockdown/Delos month; speaks to **Scary Dude** in the long dark — flow, disappear, survive.
- **M5 Seneca** (dying well, the ledger of a life) + **Gautama Buddha** (suffering as current) + **Socrates** returning for the cup.
- Cameos as bonds deepen: **Epicurus** (the McMuffin/small-pleasure scene — friendship over fortune), **Zeno** (shipwreck as beginning), **Confucius** (rites, the father's table), **Marcus** recurring.

## Five movements (map to five in-game weeks)

Each movement = one in-game week: daily 1-3-5 loop in Timeline A by day, a descent into Athens by night, closing on a Sunday Examen where the two trials are weighed together. Emotion-sprite notes name the **shift** that carries each beat.

### Movement I — THE CHARGE (Oct 2019 → the premonition)
- **A · South Florida, dawn.** Mario at the family restaurant; the ominous "something is coming, prepare." Grandfather's decline. *Sprite: Socrates `contemplation` → `doubt` as the device first lights.*
- **Threshold · The Apsu.** Enki: "I have heard your kind before. The trouble is new only to you." First bucket lowered. *Enki `teaching`.*
- **B · Athens, the stoa.** Mario arrives as Juror #501; the indictment is read — impiety, corrupting the youth. Meets Meletus's certainty. *Socrates `authority` holding steady; Meletus `conviction` (the zealot's, not the sage's).*
- **A · The recruiter's pitch.** Deacon offers the golden ticket. First **dilemma choice** (tradition-graded). *Deacon `urgency`; the Ticker `defiance`.*
- **Examen I.** Enki weighs the week: *what is the charge they'll really try you for?* Mario writes the first line of his own indictment. *Socrates `teaching`.*

### Movement II — THE APOLOGY (2020, COVID hits; the sinking dining room)
- **B · The Apology, part 1.** Socrates refuses to grovel — the gadfly, the unexamined life. He holds **`teaching`** the entire defense; the jury wants fear and he gives them clarity.
- **A · COVID lands.** The restaurant empties. Mario setting up a dining room for guests who won't come. *Scary Dude foregrounded; sprite register goes `resolve` over `grief`.* Epictetus speaks to the Waiter: the chains were never the job.
- **A · The father breaks.** The night he sees his father cry over debt. **The letter.** *This is the anti-accuser beat — Socrates alone / Mario held. Sprite: `sorrow` → `devotion`.*
- **B · The Apology, part 2 + the vote.** Mario as #501 votes to acquit. The count comes back **guilty, 280–221.** His stone meant nothing. *Socrates shifts `teaching` → `acceptance` — NOT `sorrow`. THE THESIS BEAT.*
- **Examen II.** "A jury can be wrong and still be the jury." First Legendary quote unlocked (Marcus's "to bear this worthily is good fortune").

### Movement III — THE COUNTER-PROPOSAL (2021, the meme-stock war)
- **B · The penalty phase.** Socrates counter-proposes free meals in the Prytaneum — he asks for *honor* where they expect contrition. *Socrates `honor`.*
- **A · The war.** The market-believer arc: Mario is convinced the powerful obfuscate the truth for profit. The wins arrive — twice, life-changing sums. **The Crito dilemma (major branch point):** Tani begs him to take it and walk. *Tani `compassion`; Mario `conviction`.*
- **A · The refusal.** He declines the escape — stays for a reform that won't come. *"Agárrate los huevos y acuérdate quién sos." Sprite: `defiance` → `resolve`.*
- **A · The counter-proposal, literalized.** Against the world demanding his contrition, Mario counter-proposes a **seat at the table** — stages it at his father's restaurant, cooking instead of apologizing. *`authority` earned, not claimed.*
- **Examen III.** Enki: "You asked for a seat where they wanted your knees." The Prytaneum ↔ the family table locks in.

### Movement IV — THE LONG DARK (2022–2023, the Delos month; mother's diagnosis)
- **B · The month in the cell.** The sacred ship sails to Delos; Socrates cannot be executed until it returns. A month of waiting. *Socrates `serenity` / `peace`.*
- **A · Lockdown-of-the-soul.** The long dark of the sinking dining room mapped to the cell's month. Laozi & Zhuangzi: water finds its way past every stone; the mother's diagnosis. *Time-as-adversary. Sprite: `grief` held, `serenity` sought.*
- **A · Xanthippe.** The mother: "I am ashamed, but I understand." *`sorrow` ↔ `compassion` in the same scene — the braid.*
- **B · Crito comes to the cell.** The Laws speech: Socrates won't flee the city whose laws he lived by. *Socrates `resolve`.* Mario overlays his own: he won't cheat the market he's trying to fix. *The two Critos rhyme in one screen.*
- **Examen IV.** Buddha cameo — suffering is a current, not a verdict. The blackgaze-and-electronic night-music motif; the nameless monster-movie line surfaces: **"my war isn't over."**

### Movement V — THE HEMLOCK & THE ROOSTER (2024→2026; the debt paid)
- **THE INTERNAL TRIBUNAL (set piece — see below).** Before the cup, the five masks convene; the Innocent Child prosecutes the current self.
- **B · Phaedo, the cup.** Socrates drinks. Calm. The friends weep; he steadies them. *Socrates `acceptance` → `transcendence`.* His last words: **"we owe a rooster to Asclepius" — the debt of the cured.**
- **A · The hemlock, swallowed.** Mario accepts what he fought: the reform never came; the market makers were never taken down; dignity was robbed by design. *Not defeat — diagnosis. Sprite: `acceptance`.* "Guilty of thinking conspiracies were true; guilty of being an optimist" — reframed as **cured of illusion, not of hope.**
- **A · The rooster, literalized.** Mario cooks the rooster at his father's restaurant — pays Asclepius's debt with an actual dish: gratitude for being cured. Brandon watches, and is the one who will carry it. **Final Legendary quote collected.** *Enki `devotion`; father `joy`; Mario `liberation`.*
- **Post-credits thesis.** "My war isn't over" — but it continues now through the witness and through what he builds. The device goes dark; the restaurant light stays on.

## Interactive layer

- **Dilemma choices** live in Timeline A's daily loop, tradition-graded (Stoic / Taoist / Epicurean / Socratic answers) — they shape which **philosopher bonds** deepen, unlocking deeper dialogue tiers and rarer quotes.
- **Timeline B never branches** — the juror vote is offered and overridden every time; history is fixed and that is the teaching.
- **What branches:** Mario's **verdict on himself** and the depth of each bond. Three ending bands, all "hope earned the hard way":
  1. **The Guerrilla** (high Stoic/defiance path) — the war explicitly continues; hardest, most defiant close.
  2. **The Craftsman** (high Taoist/Enki + father-bond path) — he stops fighting the ocean and builds the boat; the restaurant survives as the quiet victory.
  3. **The Witness** (high bond with Brandon/Plato) — he accepts he may not see reform, but Brandon will; the story becomes a torch passed.
- **Collection meta:** the 2,801 quotes as rarity-tiered drops; the rooster quote is the final Legendary, earnable only by completing the internal tribunal.

## The internal tribunal (set piece)

Opens Movement V, before the hemlock. The Apsu reshapes into a courtroom of five seats. **The Innocent Child is plaintiff**, accusing the current self of the mistakes made against him — the years spent, the family made to wait, the optimism that cost. **The Shamed One testifies for the prosecution** ("we were never enough"). **The Waiter and the Builder** — the two at war — must, for once, sit on the same side of the aisle to defend. Enki presides but does not judge; the player does, choosing which self gives the closing. There is no acquittal-or-conviction binary — the verdict is *mercy*: **"misericordia est virtus fortium"** — mercy is the virtue of the strong. The child drops the charge; the selves are not one, but they stop being at war. Completing it unlocks the final Legendary and the rooster scene.

## Hemlock handling (the tonal needle)

The game is fundamentally hopeful self-improvement, so death is staged as **teaching, not tragedy**: Socrates's calm is the lesson (`acceptance` → `transcendence`, never `grief` on his own face — the grief is on the friends). The hemlock in Timeline A is **metaphorical** — swallowing a truth, not dying — and it is immediately answered by the rooster: the cup takes life, the dish gives thanks. "We owe a rooster to Asclepius" reframed from morbid puzzle to the story's warmest beat: *you pay the healer because you were cured.* Cured of illusion; kept your hope. The father lives, the restaurant light stays on, and the war passes to Brandon. Nobody real dies; the death is Socrates's, and it is the most alive he ever is.
